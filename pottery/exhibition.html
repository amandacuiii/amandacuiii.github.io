Liminal

A Record of Creation In Between Stages

June 13, 2026 · Taichung

scroll

During a year spent far from home, pottery became the way I stayed connected — to the people I love, to my own cultural identity, and to the present moment. From imprinted leaf plates to Qipao vases adorned with jade, each piece served as a point of exploration and connection.

Ceramics is shaped by a series of transformations. The process involves irreversible shifts where control transfers from the artist to the material, and finally to the heat of the kiln. This craft became a mirror for a year centered on process rather than outcome, teaching me to trust what emerges. I also found myself changing and growing into different versions of myself.

The sections throughout this space each explore a different threshold. I invite you to wander, read, and reflect on your own meaning of liminal.

Time

for the time being

Inspired by Ruth Ozeki's novel, A Tale For The Time Being, this exhibit is about learning to be present: to exist fully in a moment and to find meaning in time that might slip away unnoticed.

I came to Taiwan on a Fulbright fellowship the year after I graduated. Being granted this time felt like a gift — an open stretch of days to explore the world and grow into a fuller version of myself. I wanted to use it intentionally.

Being in the studio suspended ordinary time. Hours would collapse into minutes, or stretch indefinitely. For the first time in a while, my mind was completely still. I was present.

These creations are a testament to time well spent.

Stages of Becoming

Form

Bisque firing is the first transformation in a ceramic piece's life. It exists in an in-between state: changed but not yet complete.

This is my 100th piece. Rather than finishing it myself, I want it to be shaped by everyone who walks through this space. Decorate this vase with underglaze; it will be glazed and fired again after the exhibition.

Home

is not a place, but a feeling

There's something strange about building a life somewhere you know you'll leave soon. I spent this year forming community, establishing routines, and creating my own version of Taichung, even while knowing that it was all temporary.

I started calling my apartment on Yongchang Road "home" earlier than I expected. Similarly, I felt this sense of familiarity and comfort at the studio, in my workplace, and when visiting friends. Home, I realized, will always be defined by the people who share that space with me.

In honor of finding home here, I created a series of mugs depicting my apartment on Yongchang Road and the house I grew up in back in America. Mugs are home.

Thank you to everyone who helped make this place feel like home.

Asia

belonging

Growing up Cantonese American, I assumed that coming to Asia would bring me closer to that part of myself. Instead, the opposite happened. My Americanness became the loudest thing about me, and I found myself somewhere between two identities, belonging fully to neither.

The Qipao and lucky knot became the forms through which I explored that in-between. By integrating red string and jade into vases, I tied together my Chinese heritage, my American upbringing, and the person I was becoming in Taiwan.

Taiwan exists in its own kind of in-between: a place that holds space for tradition and modernity, contradiction and coexistence, and whose status as a country remains unresolved. Living here as a foreigner and a witness, I saw how much culture can thrive within uncertainty. Belonging can exist even in the most unresolved of places.

Stages of Becoming

Mark

The leaf plates were made by pressing real leaves into clay, transforming the organic and temporary into a permanent mark. The impression that remains is its own kind of liminal object, suspending a living thing in between nature and artifice.

Here, you can try the same process on paper using ceramic stamps of Taiwan leaves.

Arts

I am not an artist, I just make art

This exhibition is the first time I've ever written an artist statement. As someone who studied finance and only recently started creating, I struggled to claim the identity of an "artist". The theme of this exhibition captures how I felt throughout my artistic journey this past year: in a constant state of becoming, discovery, and wonder.

While preparing for this show, I kept returning to memories of my mother and grandmother, watching them garden, make zongzi, and knit scarves. From them, I learned to create with intention and to use craft as a way of connecting with the people around me. I owe my love for art to them.

"Long before we called ourselves artists or crafters or creatives, we stitched, carved, wove, painted. We told stories in patterns, in pigments, in pots and cloth. We shaped the world around us with our hands; out of need, because of beauty, out of love. It's a part of being human, this impulse to create."
— Emily Charlotte Powell
