Liminal

A record of creation between stages

2024 – 2025

scroll

Clay begins as nothing — rough, formless, indifferent. Each piece in this exhibition started the same way: a lump of earth, shaped by hand across weeks of studio time.

Liminal is a record of the in-between. Not the idea, not the outcome — the threshold. Between raw material and finished object. Between making something and letting it go.

01 — raw clay

The beginning

It starts with a block of earth, still cold from the studio shelf. Nothing about it suggests what it will become.

02 — taking shape

Form

Pinching, rolling, coiling. The silhouette appears before the details do. Hands work by feel as much as intention.

03 — building

Structure

Walls smoothed and thinned. Some changes are deliberate; others are accidents kept.

04 — detail

Carving

Detail added by subtraction. A loop tool traces the pattern — careful, then less careful, then careful again.

05 — waiting

Drying

The piece rests. Clay needs time to stiffen before it can go into the kiln. This part cannot be rushed.

06 — glaze

Color

After bisque firing, glaze is painted on. The color looks nothing like the finished surface — it reveals itself only in the fire.

07 — finished

Qipao (jade)

Six stages, several weeks. This is what was always inside the clay.

Plant Pot · in progress

Brushstrokes

Each marking made in one pass — ink-brush strokes translated into glaze. The gesture has to commit.

Walls thinned, edges refined. A plant pot in form, but the surface carries the weight of the piece.

The strokes are painted. Against the unfired clay they look flat. The kiln will change that.

After firing, the glaze has settled into something quieter than expected. That too is the piece now.

Bowl · in progress

Fruits of Your Labor

A bowl meant to hold things — fruit, small objects, a little nothing. The title came after.

Clay coiled and smoothed. The rim pulled outward, then corrected, then left slightly imperfect.

Surface textures pressed in by hand. Marks that will survive the firing.

Glaze layered. The colors sit wet and dull on the bisqueware — the transformation is still ahead.
