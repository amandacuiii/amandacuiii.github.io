How It Started

In February 2026, I traveled to Guangfu Township, Hualien, for an Indigenous pottery camp led by my ceramics teacher, Papay. He is part of the Amis tribe, and he started these camps as a way to share knowledge about the Amis and about Indigenous life in Taiwan. He invited my sister and me to join.

While there, we learned about Amis culture and what ceramics mean to the community, and we made our own pieces from clay dug out of the earth. Learning from local elders, I came to understand why the rituals matter, and what it means to honor the land you live on.

I documented my experience on Instagram, and the response was an outpouring of enthusiasm, intrigue, and excitement. Many people asked how they could come to Taiwan and experience it themselves.

So I collaborated with Papay to launch an International Pottery Camp, taught with English translation, to make traditional Amis ceramic education accessible to students around the world. In July 2026, we hosted the first camp with 16 students from 8 countries.

Sa'Atomo

Guangfu, Hualien · July 2026
In July 2026, I partnered with Austronesian Alien Studio to bring 16 people from 8 different countries to join us in Guangfu, Hualien. Over five days we learned from members of the Indigenous Amis tribe, dug our own clay from the earth, and wedged it by hand (and by foot). We gathered our own firewood and built a pit fire that reached 1,000 degrees Celsius. Most importantly, we got to really know one another.
Pottery became a shared language for us, and a medium for connection, creativity, and craft.
Thank you to the 16 individuals who made the first cohort unforgettable: Aaliyah, Alicia, Angel, Cassie, Catharine, Chung, Dan, Hailey, Han, Jianna, Kimberlyn, Lee Rong, Nicole, Samantha, Natasha, and Tessa.

What We Did Over The 5 Days
Day 01
Gathering in Guangfu

The group came together in Guangfu and started straight away on traditional Amis pots, learning from Amma, an elder in the tribe.

Day 02
Driftwood, Soil and Workshops

We collected driftwood to begin building our fire pit and gathered soil to wedge into clay. The day also took in a local museum and artists' studios, alongside pottery and educational workshops led by members of the tribe.

The Firing Begins

We started the Amis pottery firing, made our own tools, and took turns tending the fire.

Day 04
Tree Bark Art and Group Creation

A tree bark art session and an Amis plants workshop, both led by a local Indigenous group. Then a group creation, building large Amis vessels together.

Day 05
Exploring Guangfu

We spent the last day exploring Guangfu, visiting the flood museum and a local wood firing spot.

About the Camp

"Sa'atomo" (阿美陶部落營隊) means "come make pottery" in Amis. The camp is held in Guangfu, one of the cultural heartlands of the Amis, Taiwan's largest indigenous group. Clay is gathered by hand from the earth, shaped by pinching and coiling rather than thrown on a wheel, and fired in an open pit.

Background on Guangfu, Hualien, Taiwan

Guangfu (光復) is a small township on the east coast of Taiwan in Hualien County, nestled between the Central Mountain Range and the Pacific Ocean.

Guangfu was impacted by significant flooding in 2025. The studio and camp operations are continuing, and attending this camp is also a meaningful way to support the local community during its recovery. We will be visiting a local exhibition documenting the flood's impact on the local residents.

About Austronesian Alien Studio (南島星人工作室)

Austronesian Alien Studio is an indigenous-led creative studio based in Taichung, Taiwan, dedicated to preserving and sharing Amis cultural practices through hands-on education.

The studio was founded by Papay, a pottery teacher and cultural educator who started Austronesian Alien with the goal of expanding access to indigenous culture, creating a space where both indigenous youth and visitors from around the world could learn traditional Amis pottery in its living context.

I started my ceramics journey here.

Activities Include
Soil Gathering Ritual
素材蒐集

The camp begins not at a workbench but in the field. We will travel to Fengbin to gather raw clay directly from the earth, participating in the Amis ritual that marks the start of the pottery process.

Dig & Work the Clay
挖土練土

Learn to prepare raw gathered clay by hand: cleaning, kneading, and conditioning it into workable material. This is the foundation of traditional Amis pottery, done entirely without a wheel.

Hand-Building & Sculpting
陶器創作

Using pinching and coiling techniques, you'll build your own pieces across multiple sessions, guided by indigenous artisans and elders. Afternoon and night sessions are dedicated to independent practice and developing your work.

Wild Pit Firing + Tool Making
陶器野燒 + 工具製作

Rather than a kiln, Amis pottery is fired in an open pit, an ancient technique that produces distinctive, unpredictable results. You'll also make your own tools for the firing process. On pit firing night, participants take turns tending the fire on a rotating overnight watch.

Plant Workshop
植物工作坊

Explore the role of local plants and natural materials in Amis cultural and pottery practices.

Spirit Workshop
靈觀工作坊

An evening session rooted in Amis spiritual traditions, offering context for the rituals woven throughout the camp.

Group Creation
集體創作

A collaborative build where the group works on one piece together, mirroring how Amis pottery has always been made: communally, not in isolation.

Tree Bark Art
樹皮布工藝

A hands-on introduction to bark cloth, beaten from paper mulberry into a workable material, one of the oldest Amis crafts still practiced today.

Eating GOOOD FOOD!!

Every meal is shared. Cooking and eating together is as much a part of the camp as the pottery.

A deposit of 30% of the total tuition ($300 USD) is required to enroll and secure your spot. Instructions on paying your deposit will follow after you fill out the enrollment form. The rest is paid on or before the first day of class, and full payment instructions come with your acceptance notice.
Travel and Visa

Roundtrip transportation from Guangfu Station to the studio can be arranged for people traveling from Taipei, for an additional $50 USD. A member of the studio will meet participants in Taipei on the day of departure to travel together to Guangfu.

Otherwise, the nearest major train station is Guangfu Station (光復車站), and we will pick you up from the station when you arrive.

A note on weather: January in Guangfu is cool and damp. Expect daytime highs around 20 to 22°C (68 to 72°F) and morning lows of 13 to 15°C (55 to 59°F). Hualien winters are generally comfortable, windy and cloudy, with temperatures rarely dropping below 12°C.
