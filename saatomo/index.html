How It Started

In February 2026, I traveled to Guangfu Township, Hualien, for an Indigenous pottery camp led by my ceramics teacher.

While there, we dug and prepared the clay, built by hand, and fired in an open pit.

I documented my experience on Instagram, and the response was an outpouring of enthusiasm, intrigue, and excitement — with many people asking how they could come to Taiwan and experience it themselves.

So I collaborated with Papay to launch an International Pottery Camp, taught with English translation, to make traditional Amis ceramic education accessible to students around the world. In July 2026, we hosted the first camp with 16 students from 8 countries.

The Place

Guangfu (光復) is a township on Taiwan's east coast in Hualien County, between the Central Mountain Range and the Pacific. It is one of the cultural heartlands of the Amis (阿美族), Taiwan's largest indigenous group.

The Amis have kept a living tradition of pottery tied to the land for generations. The process is inseparable from its cultural and spiritual context — from the rituals around gathering soil to the communal nature of the firing. Pottery here is a form of cultural memory.

The Studio

Sa'Atomo (阿美陶部落營隊) means "come make pottery" in Amis. The camp is run by Austronesian Alien Studio (南島星人工作室), an indigenous-led studio founded by Papay, a pottery teacher and cultural educator.

The studio's aim is to widen access to indigenous culture — a place where both indigenous youth and visitors can learn traditional Amis pottery in its living context, not as a museum artifact but as a practiced tradition.

What We Did
  • Gathered clay directly from the earth
  • Cleaned, kneaded and conditioned it by hand
  • Collected and stacked firewood
  • Built vessels by pinching and coiling
  • Dug and packed the open firing pit
  • Fired overnight, then made the offering

About the Camp

"Sa'atomo" (阿美陶部落營隊) means "come make pottery" in Amis. The camp is held in Guangfu, one of the cultural heartlands of the Amis, Taiwan's largest indigenous group. Clay is gathered by hand from the earth, shaped by pinching and coiling rather than thrown on a wheel, and fired in an open pit.

The camp emphasises process over product — each step carries its cultural context, from the land offering ritual to what the firing means. It is run with Austronesian Alien Studio, an indigenous-led studio founded by Papay, and taught alongside Amis artisans and elders.

What You'll Do
  • Soil gathering ritual (素材蒐集)
  • Dig and work the clay (挖土練土)
  • Hand-building & sculpting (陶器創作)
  • Wild pit firing + tool making (陶器野燒)
  • Plant workshop (植物工作坊)
  • Spirit workshop (靈觀工作坊)
  • Music & dance workshop (樂舞工作坊)
  • Tree bark art
